BRIEF — TOOLS-38: How to Automate Blog Creation With AI
Status: ready for drafting and independent source review. This is a brief, not an approved article or evidence of source verification.

Assignment
Write a practical English article for small marketing teams, independent publishers and business owners who know blogging but are new to automated workflows. Aim for 1,500–2,000 words as an editorial target, not an SEO rule. Give readers a repeatable path from idea to human decision. Use plain language and a concrete, hypothetical example. Do not assert traffic, rankings, savings, search demand or algorithm effects without approved, verified evidence. Search demand, target site, market, conversion and Search Console data are unavailable; do not invent them. Keep production instructions and internal notes out of public copy.

Reader outcome
A reader can define a bounded assignment, assemble an approved source set, use AI to assist with an outline and draft, verify each factual claim, quotation and citation, resolve rights and evidence gaps, and route a versioned package to a person for a documented decision. Treat automation as assistance, with a named person accountable for editorial decisions. Describe this as the recommended workflow, not an empirical promise.

Proposed structure
1. Introduce the workflow and its boundaries. Distinguish assistance with organizing, outlining and drafting from human accountability for accuracy, rights, relevance and approval.
2. Choose an idea and define the audience, purpose, scope, exclusions and intended decision. Explain that no unprovided demand data is available.
3. Create a bounded brief and approved source set. Give each source an ID, URL, version or retrieval date and permitted scope. Exclude unsupported material; record missing evidence as a blocker.
4. Generate an outline and draft using only the approved brief and source set. Ask for claim-to-source references and visible gaps; do not treat generated citations as verified.
5. Verify every factual claim, quotation and citation against the actual source, including context and exact quotation. Record evidence and version; correct or remove unsupported statements. Block missing evidence and unresolved rights. A source reviewer must fetch the approved URLs and check contents before claims are accepted.
6. Review for usefulness, scope, readability, rights and consistency. Show how a person records corrections and the final approve, revise or reject decision. Preserve linked versions of brief, sources, draft, review, channel assets, package and decision.
7. Include the reusable template, illustrative prompts, tracker example and checklist below, adapted into reader-facing copy.
