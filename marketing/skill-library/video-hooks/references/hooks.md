# Hooks

A hook works by opening a gap the viewer needs closed, then the video closes it. The gap must be real. Build the hook from the strongest true element of the video (the most surprising fact, the highest stake, the most specific number, the sharpest opinion, the most relatable moment), then pick the mechanism that exposes it.

## 1. Rules that hold for every hook
- Confirm the title's promise in the first sentence. A hook that ignores its own title is why people leave at 0:08.
- One idea. A hook carrying two promises keeps neither.
- Specific beats big: "400 to 200,000 in seven months" beats "massive growth". Odd, exact numbers read as measured; round ones read as invented.
- Show the artifact early if you promised one (inside 20 seconds on long-form, inside the first frame on short-form).
- Never open with who you are, a greeting, a logo, or a subscribe pitch. None of it has been earned yet.
- Write the hook before the script. If you cannot write three credible hooks in 60 seconds, the idea is weak: cut or reframe it.
- Always deliver a batch of 5 to 10 across at least three different mechanisms, then keep the best two. One hook is a guess.

## 2. The 21 formulas
| # | Formula | Shape | Example | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Statistic** | A number the viewer did not know, stated flat, then the consequence. | [A number from the brief] of the people who start a channel quit before video 30. Here is what the rest do differently. | Dies if the number is vague or unverifiable. Say the source out loud. |
| 2 | **Someone Else's Result** | A named person's outcome, then the mechanism. | This channel went from 400 subscribers to 200,000 in seven months on one format. I mapped it. | Do not invent the number. If you cannot name the channel, use a different formula. |
| 3 | **The Mistake** | Name the error the viewer is probably making right now. | Your first 30 seconds are why nobody finishes your videos. It is not the topic. | Only works if the mistake is common and the viewer can check it in one second. |
| 4 | **Contrarian Flip** | State the accepted advice, then reject it. | Everyone says post daily. I posted once a week for a year and tripled the channel. | Needs evidence in the next 15 seconds or it reads as contrarian for its own sake. |
| 5 | **The Reveal** | Promise a specific thing will be shown, then show it. | I am going to show you the exact prompt, on screen, that wrote this video. | You have to actually show it, early. A reveal at 8:00 is a retention cliff at 1:00. |
| 6 | **The Superlative** | The single best/worst/fastest of a category. | This is the fastest way to turn one long video into thirty Shorts. | Superlatives are cheap. Earn it by naming what it beat. |
| 7 | **The Clock** | Bound the payoff in time. | In the next six minutes you will have a working title, thumbnail and script for your next video. | The clock has to be true. Overrun it and the next video's hook is not believed. |
| 8 | **I Tried It** | First-person experiment with a stated cost. | I spent a full week on one script and cut it to half the length. | Needs a real cost - time, money, risk - or there is no stake. |
| 9 | **The Question** | Ask the exact question the viewer typed into search. | Why do your videos die at 30 seconds when the topic is fine? | Must be the viewer's question, not yours. Pull it from your own comments. |
| 10 | **Before and After** | Two states, one cut between them. | This was my thumbnail. This is my thumbnail now. Same video, four times the click-through. | Only works with a visual. Do not use it on a talking-head-only video. |
| 11 | **The Teardown** | Take a real thing apart in public. | I pulled the nine most-viewed AI videos of the month and broke down what the titles share. | Name what you pulled and how many. A teardown of one example is an anecdote. |
| 12 | **The Stack** | Two named tools combined into one outcome. | A spreadsheet plus your analytics export gives you next week's upload schedule in one prompt. | Both tools must be nameable and the outcome must need both. |
| 13 | **The Warning** | A cost the viewer is about to pay. | Do not upload another video until you check this one setting. | If the cost is small the hook is a lie. Reserve it. |
| 14 | **The List** | A counted set, with the count in the first line. | Seven things I would do differently if I started a channel in 2026. | The count must be exact and every item must be different in kind. |
| 15 | **The Receipt** | Show the artifact first, explain second. | This is the analytics page. This video did 40% of the channel's watch time. Here is why. | Requires a real screenshot. A described receipt is not a receipt. |
| 16 | **The Insider** | Information from inside a system. | YouTube tells you which videos are underperforming. Almost nobody opens the report. | Do not claim insider access you do not have. |
| 17 | **The Impossible Claim** | State something that sounds untrue, then prove it. | You can write, title, thumbnail and schedule a week of videos without opening a single editor. | The proof has to start within 20 seconds or it reads as clickbait. |
| 18 | **The Comparison** | Two named options, one winner. | I wrote the same video three ways. One of them held 62% and the other two did not. | Say the losing option out loud. A comparison with no loser is an advert. |
| 19 | **The Origin** | Where a result actually came from. | Every video on this channel starts in the same 12-line file. Here it is. | Works once per channel. It is a reveal about you, and you only have one. |
| 20 | **The Deadline** | Something is changing on a date. | The old description format stops mattering this month. Here is what replaces it. | Only use with a real, checkable date. This is the fastest formula to lose trust with. |
| 21 | **The Direct Address** | Name the exact viewer in the first six words. | If you have under a thousand subscribers, this is the only video you need this week. | Narrow beats broad. 'If you make videos' addresses nobody. |

## 3. Named-creator archetypes
Mix them. The point is variety across a batch, not purity inside one hook.

**Kallaway's six** (a reusable menu; any idea plugs into several)
1. Fortuneteller: "This [thing] is about to change how [audience] [does action]." Needs a real demo.
2. Experimenter: "I did [X] for [time] to find out [Y]." Visible effort buys credibility.
3. Teacher: "[Person] hit [result] using [unexpected method]." Specific outcome, named method.
4. Magician: "Look at this. That's actually a [surprise]." The visual carries it; the line is a pointer.
5. Investigator: "I found a [hidden thing] in [familiar place] that does [unexpected thing]." Safer baseline, fewer flops.
6. Contrarian: "Everyone thinks [X] because [Y]. They're wrong." Highest ceiling, highest variance; the payoff must defend the claim.

**Other frameworks worth borrowing**
- MrBeast's contract: the first sentence validates the title, the first shot validates the thumbnail. Escalate immediately; the first minute gets disproportionate planning time.
- Hormozi: hook (under 3s) → retain (concrete content, no filler) → reward (something usable). Title stem: "How to [desired result] without [pain point]."
- Brunson/Kern: hook → story → offer. Strong for paid and warm audiences; soften the offer on cold organic.
- Koe (APAG): the attention stage passes three filters: relevance, a concrete benefit, and awareness match.
- Welsh: three short lines that end on a teaser; stem "X lessons/things/mistakes I wish I knew before I [did Y]."
- Bush: lead with concrete numbers; expand one claim through 13 vectors (tips, stats, steps, stories, quotes, benefits, lessons, reasons, mistakes, examples, questions, resources, frameworks). Ship cheap hook attempts and expand only the winners.
- Galloway: click-to-unpause. Compose the first frame as if paused mid-action so the viewer's instinct is to press play.

**Picking one**
- Visual payoff or product result → Magician, or in-medias-res.
- How-to → Teacher, Hormozi's "How to X without Y", or Investigator.
- Defensible opinion → Contrarian or Fortuneteller (only if the payoff defends it).
- Long or expensive effort → Experimenter or a big-number lead.
- Cold paid audience → hook-story-offer, offer softened.

## 4. Tactics that upgrade a flat draft
Apply in this order; if three still leave it flat, the angle is the problem, not the wording.
1. Add a number (odd and exact).
2. Add an authority signal in the first half-second (only if real).
3. Name the exact viewer ("If you have under 1,000 subscribers…"). Narrow beats broad.
4. Cut the first three words; most hooks bury the lede.
5. Swap weak verbs for power verbs ("burned" not "spent", "cracked" not "figured out", "ditched" not "got rid of").
6. Add a contradiction: state the consensus, then break it.
7. Switch to a withheld reveal.

Extra moves: a **shaped curiosity gap** names the category of the answer without giving it ("I tried every cold brew maker under $50. One actually works") where a vague tease ("you won't believe this") reads as bait. A **cost confession** ("I spent $413 so you can skip to the $39 one") works only with a real receipt. **In-medias-res**: start inside the action, explain after. **Staccato**: two or three short clauses, drop connective filler ("and", "so", "basically"). One visual change inside the hook window resets attention.

## 5. The three-layer hook (video)
In the first 1 to 2 seconds, three layers must agree, and the hook must work muted.
- **Visual**: the payoff, the conflict, or a mid-action frame. Never a static face waiting to start, a logo, or a fade-in from black.
- **Verbal**: 5 to 10 words, payoff or stake first, one claim said once.
- **On-screen text**: 3 to 7 words that compress and sharpen the spoken line (drop verbs and articles, keep numbers and the contrast: "$400 → $39"). Do not repeat the spoken sentence.
- Optional fourth: audio timing (beat drop on the first cut, cut to silence on the reveal).
Test: cover the text, does the visual still hook? Cover the visual, does the text alone earn a pause? Both must pass. Write the verbal line first, design the visual to land before the voice, write the text last.

## 6. Opening beats: long-form
Grab → promise → stakes, in the first 30 to 40 seconds.

## 7. Anti-patterns (name the pattern, then rewrite)
Welcome intro / logo sting; vague tease; bait-and-switch; subscribe-before-value; story opener that starts with chronology; "today we're going to talk about…"; imperative commands ("stop scrolling") the payoff cannot earn; hook bolted on after the script; single-layer hook (voice only); brand-first opener; cleverness that needs decoding; slow build (the real hook is sentence three: delete the first two); a three-act hook that takes 10 seconds to resolve; throat-clearing ("so I've been thinking…").

## 8. Pre-flight checklist (pass/fail, run on the final two)
- Does a stranger need the next beat? (Pull: fail = not a hook yet.)
- Will the video pay it off? (Truth: fail = disqualified however good it sounds.)
- Specific noun, number or claim?
- Resolves inside the format's window (long-form ~30s, short-form ~3s)?
- Works muted?
- Sounds like the creator (voice profile / banned words respected)?
