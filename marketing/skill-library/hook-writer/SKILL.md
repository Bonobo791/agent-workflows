# Hook craft: the opening that earns attention

The opening line is the most important line of a social post. A reader decides within a second whether to keep reading. A good opening is true to what follows.

1. A hook opens a gap the reader needs to close. Use one mechanism: a surprising fact, a stake the reader cares about, a specific number or detail, a sharp point of view, a moment the reader recognises, or a clear promise of what they will learn.
2. Take the hook from the content itself. Look for the strongest true element: the most surprising result, the highest cost, the most specific detail, the boldest claim the piece can back up. Build the hook from that element.
3. Never overpromise. The hook must be paid off by the text that follows. A hook that promises more than the post delivers loses the reader and their trust.
4. Write the opening in the brand's voice. Avoid stock openings such as "Excited to share", "Here's the thing" and "Stop scrolling".
5. Write several candidate openings, each using a different mechanism. Score each against the checks below. Keep the strongest one and discard the rest.
6. Fit the opening to where it appears. The first line is the only line most readers see before they decide, so it must stand alone.

Checks before the opening is final:
- Does it open a gap that a stranger needs to close?
- Is it specific: a real detail, figure or claim from the content?
- Is it true, and does the content pay it off?
- Does it sound like the brand?
- Would a reader who saw only this line want the next one?

Return the single strongest opening. Do not return a list of options.
