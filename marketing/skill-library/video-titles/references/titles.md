# Titles

The title is half the click. Write it as a pair with the thumbnail and the opening hook: the title sets the gap, the thumbnail shows the artifact or emotion, the first line confirms the promise.

## 1. Formulas
| Code | Formula | Skeleton | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Curiosity gap | "{Surprising result}. Here is what nobody tells you." / "Why {common thing} {fails} the opposite way you think" | a surprising outcome with a hidden mechanism |
| T2 | Number / listicle | "{N} {things} that {payoff} (number {X} {surprised me})" | a finite, scannable payoff |
| T3 | How-I outcome | "How I {specific result with a number} in {timeframe}" | first-person proof; odd precision ("$24,937") pulls harder than round |
| T4 | Mistake / loss | "Stop {action}. You are {losing/wasting} {specific thing}." | gear and tutorial niches; fix must arrive fast |
| T5 | Transformation | "I {did hard thing} for {timeframe}. Here is what changed." | before/after arcs; needs a real turn |
| T6 | Versus | "{A} vs {B}: I {did the same thing} in both" | search-friendly named products |
| T7 | Third-party success | "How {named person} built {big number} from zero" | aspiration through a proxy; number and story must be checkable |

Niche note (one 214-video business/creator pull, July 2026, source-reported): T1, T3, T5 were the confirmed over-performers; pure listicles (T2), loss framing (T4) and versus (T6) were rare there. Treat T2, T4, T6 as niche-dependent, not universal.

Stems borrowed from creators: "How to [desired result] without [pain point]" (Hormozi); "X mistakes I wish I knew before I [did Y]" (Welsh); "I [did X] for [time] to find out [Y]" (Experimenter).

## 2. Hard rules
- YouTube's hard cap is 100 characters, but this pipeline allows at most 60. Aim 40 to 60: desktop search cuts near 60, a mobile home feed near 40. Put the click-deciding words before any colon.
- A concrete noun plus curiosity. Never a dry label.
- One specific number, name or date beats every adjective.
- "How I" beats "How to" when there is a lived result.
- Title and thumbnail never repeat words: different words, same promise.
- At most two ALL-CAPS words; 0 to 1 emoji; no em dash (a colon reads cleaner).
- Search phrase appears once, naturally. No keyword stuffing.
- Strip AI tells from a one-line title: "leverage", "game-changer", "deep dive", "unlock", reveal bridges ("here's what nobody tells you" as filler), sincerity framing ("honestly?", "real talk").
## 3. Browse vs search
| The video is… | Lead with | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| broad-appeal, feed-driven | curiosity + emotion | "I posted a Short every day for 30 days" |
| how-to / tutorial | the exact search phrase | "How to color grade in DaVinci Resolve (fast)" |
| comparison / decision | the two named options | "Final Cut vs DaVinci Resolve" |
| personal / story | the transformation arc | "How I got my first 1,000 subscribers" |

## 4. Pairing table
| Title | Open with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T1 curiosity gap | restate-and-raise | confirm the gap is real, then widen it |
| T2 number | question-and-contract | promise the list payoff plus a bonus |
| T3 how-I | cold-open payoff | show the result first, then the steps |
| T4 mistake | restate-and-raise | name the cost, then the fix |
| T5 transformation | cold-open payoff | flash the after, cut to the before |
| T6 versus | question-and-contract | answer "which wins" fast, then the nuance |
| T7 success story | cold-open payoff | flash the outsized result, then trace how |
| Any Short | 3-second frame-one hook | the title matters less; the first frame is everything |

## 5. Per-title checklist
Under 100 chars (ideally 40 to 60) · promise, not label · concrete noun + curiosity · one specific number where true · no thumbnail-word overlap · no AI vocabulary · no stuffing · at least 3 variants spanning at least 2 formulas.

## 6. Process
1. Gather: topic, the real payoff, target viewer, main search phrase, long-form or Short.
2. Decide browse-first or search-first.
3. Shortlist 2 to 3 formulas, draft 10 titles, and check each one by hand against sections 2 and 5.
4. Show the top 3 with their scores, formula, char count and the one issue on each; flag the one to lead A/B with (YouTube's Test & Compare).
5. Lead with the winner in the package.
