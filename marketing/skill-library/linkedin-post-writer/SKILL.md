# LinkedIn post craft

A LinkedIn post earns its reach by being read to the end and by starting a real conversation. Write it that way.

1. The opening line must make the reader tap to read more. Put the tension or the specific point in the first line. Do not warm up, and do not announce ("excited to share").
2. Format for reading. Use short sentences and short paragraphs with white space between them. Keep each paragraph to one or two lines. Do not make every line a dramatic single fragment; that reads as performance.
3. Deliver what the opening promised. Use a plain, professional voice. Make the specific point, give the evidence or the story behind it, and stop when the point is made.
4. Close with one genuine question that the reader can answer from their own work. Ask about their experience, not for agreement.
5. Write in the first person when the post is about the author's work or view. Use the author's own real experience only. Never invent a personal story; if none exists, make the point directly from the facts.
6. Do not use engagement bait: no "comment YES", no "agree?", no emoji pointing at the comments, no request to tag people.
7. Do not use fake vulnerability or staged emotion.
8. Put hashtags at the end of the post, and only the few that describe the topic.
9. Do not put a link in the body text. The contract places any link in the first comment.

Before the post is final, check: does the first line earn the tap; is the body easy to read and worth the read; does it end on a question the reader can answer; is it free of bait and staging; does the voice match the brand?
