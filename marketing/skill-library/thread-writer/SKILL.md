# Short-post craft: one idea, and a thread only when it needs one

A short post carries one point. A thread is a short series of posts that makes one point in steps. Use a thread only when the point cannot be made in one post.

1. Decide whether one post will do. If the point fits in one post, write one post. A thread padded to look bigger is worse than one strong post.
2. Keep one idea. Each post in a thread moves the same idea forward by one beat: a step, a reason, an example, or the result.
3. The first post must do two things at once. It must stop the reader and promise the payoff of the rest. It must also stand alone as a post, because it is the one people share.
4. Each later post must earn the next. Use a clear step, a short bridge such as "but here is the catch", or a question that the next post answers.
5. Cut every post that is filler, repeats an earlier post, or stands apart from the idea.
6. Close with the payoff in one sentence, then one clear request: to reply, to share, or to read the full piece.
7. Do not chop a long article into posts. Rebuild the single idea as a native thread that reads well on its own.
8. Use short lines and plain words. Keep each post readable at a glance.

Before the thread is final, check: does the first post stop the reader and promise a payoff; does each post advance one idea; does every post earn the next; does the close give the payoff and one request; would one post have done the job?
