# Accessibility craft for images and video

Make every graphic and every frame readable by people who cannot see well, cannot hear, or see in colour differently.

1. Contrast: text must stand out clearly from its background. Normal text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1. Large text and essential graphics need at least 3 to 1.
2. Colour alone must never carry meaning. If a point is marked only by colour, add a shape, a word or a position as well.
3. Text size: all text must be readable at the size a phone shows it. Keep text clear of the edges and of other text. Nothing may be cut off or overlap.
4. Reading order: the headline comes first, then the supporting line. A reader scanning the picture meets the most important words first.
5. Time on screen: keep on-screen text up long enough to read at a normal pace. Change the text only after the reader has had time to read it.
6. Movement: avoid rapid flashing or flickering in any frame.
7. Description: write a plain-language description of each image or each key frame that says what it shows and what its words say. Use it as the alt text or the description where the platform accepts one.
8. Spoken and written text: a video must make sense with the sound off. Put every key spoken point on screen as text.

Before the output is final, check: does every text pass the contrast standard; is colour never the only signal; is all text readable at phone size; is the reading order right; does each image have a description; does the video make sense with the sound off.
