# Text graphic craft: one idea, legible, honest

A text graphic is a single idea made large enough to read and worth keeping. It amplifies a line that is already strong; it cannot make a weak line strong.

1. Pick a line that stands alone. It should make sense without the article, be short, and make a reader stop. If no line does, the graphic should carry the single fact the brief gives.
2. Keep one idea per graphic. Put the key phrase first, and let everything else support it.
3. Break lines where the meaning holds. Never break a line inside a word.
4. Use at most two typefaces in one graphic.
5. Make the text large, with strong contrast against its background and generous space around it. Keep the text away from busy parts of the picture.
6. Attribute honestly. Use only words that the brief gives, and only the exact wording that the source gives. Never put invented words in a real person's mouth. Never trim a quotation so that its meaning changes.
7. Use a figure only if the brief gives it with its source.
8. Do not reproduce song lyrics, poems or other copyrighted text.
9. Test legibility at the size a phone shows it. If a reader cannot read the graphic at that size, make the text larger or use fewer words.

Colours and typefaces come from the design system block for this task, not from this skill. Do not add colour or font rules here.

Before the graphic is final, check: does it carry one idea; is the key phrase first; are the line breaks right; is the attribution exact; does it read at phone size.
