# Teaching craft: one lesson, taught completely and accurately

A lesson teaches one thing completely. It is a card a reader keeps, not a chapter they skim.

1. Choose one teachable thing: one process, one point, or one next step. If it cannot be taught well in one piece, split it into a series and teach the first part.
2. Check accuracy before writing. Every step must be correct and every claim must be supported. Never invent steps, figures or outcomes. Write only what the source material supports, and keep to what the writer actually knows.
3. For health, money or legal topics, state only what a primary source supports. Add a plain note that the piece is general information, not professional advice, and point the reader to a qualified professional for their own case.
4. Arrange the lesson in this order: an opening that says who it is for and what they will be able to do; the context in a line or two; one step or idea per unit; a short recap; one specific call to action.
5. Choose one structure and keep to it. Use step by step for a process, problem then solution for a fix, myth then correction for a misunderstanding, or the data first for a finding.
6. Make every unit earn its place. Remove any unit that repeats another or that a reader does not need to finish the lesson.
7. Write the call to action as one specific action: save the piece, send it to a named person, or ask for the next part by a stated keyword. Never give two calls to action.
8. Keep the same lesson whatever the format. The format writer decides how the lesson is laid out; the content stays the same.

Measure the lesson by how often it is saved and sent, not by likes.

Do not promise results the lesson cannot deliver, and do not present the writer's own opinion as established fact.
