# Internal linking craft

Links between pages help a reader find related pages and show a search engine which pages matter most.

1. Every page must be reachable. Give each page at least one link from related content. A page that nothing links to is nearly invisible.
2. Every page must lead somewhere. End each page with links to the next useful pages: a related article, the hub page for its topic, or the next step. Do not leave a reader at a dead end.
3. Link from the most important page to the page it supports. Prefer links in the body text, where the link is in context, over links in a footer or sidebar.
4. Use descriptive anchor text that says what the target page is about. Never use "click here", "read more" or the bare page address.
5. Keep the most important links few and clear. A page with dozens of links dilutes each one.
6. Link a page to its topic hub, and link the hub back to every page in its cluster. Cross-link related pages in the same cluster where a reader would want to go next.
7. Bring important pages close to the start. A page that a reader must click through four levels to reach is hard to find.
8. Where two pages link one way and the topic is shared, add the reverse link where it reads naturally.

For a new article, the links to plan are: the hub page for its topic, two or more related articles in the same cluster, and one page that gives the next step.

Do not add links that serve only search ranking and that a reader would not want to follow.
