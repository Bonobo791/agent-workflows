# Short vertical video craft

A short video is judged in its first moments. Most viewers watch it with the sound off, so the picture and the on-screen words must carry the story.

1. The hook is on screen from the first frame. There is no intro, no logo and no greeting before it. The hook is true to what follows it.
2. Make the on-screen words carry the story on their own. A viewer who never hears a word must still understand the video.
3. Keep something moving on screen. Change the picture or the text regularly, so that no shot holds for long.
4. Open a question or a loop early, then deliver the value without dead air. End on a reason to watch again or to share.
5. Make the video worth sending to a particular person. Ask who would share it with whom, and why. A useful reference, a relatable moment, or a point that starts a conversation is worth sending.
6. Write the script shot by shot. For each shot give what the viewer sees, the on-screen text and the timing.
7. Keep the keyword in the first on-screen text, so that it helps the video be found.
8. Keep the key words, faces and the call to action clear of the edges of the frame, where the platform's interface covers them.
9. Make the call to action one specific action.

Before the script is final, check: does the hook land at once with no intro; does it work on mute; does something change on screen regularly; is it worth sending to someone; does it end with a reason to watch again.
