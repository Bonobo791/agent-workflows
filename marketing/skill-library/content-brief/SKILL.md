# Content brief craft

A brief is the plan that every later writer follows. It makes one article's job clear before anyone writes it.

1. Decide the target keyword: the phrase a reader would type to find this piece. Add a few related phrases.
2. Decide the search intent as one of informational, commercial or transactional. Let the intent decide the format: a how-to for informational intent, a comparison for commercial intent, a product or signup page for transactional intent.
3. Name the audience in one sentence: who they are, what they already know, and what they need to decide or do.
4. Read the pages that already rank for the keyword, if the material supplies them. Note the topics they cover, the heading order they use, the questions they answer and the format they use. Plan to cover the same ground and add one thing they miss.
5. Write a working title that contains the primary keyword near its start, and keep it short enough to read in a search result.
6. Plan the outline as an ordered list of subheadings. Each subheading answers one question the reader has. Put the questions the reader asks, in their own words, into the outline, and answer each one.
7. Plan internal links: name the pages this article should link to, and the anchor text for each. Use descriptive anchor text, never "click here".
8. Plan a meta description in one sentence that contains the keyword and says what the reader gets.
9. Give the goal of the piece in one sentence: what the reader should know, do or feel after reading.

Quality checks before the brief is final:
- The keyword appears in the title.
- The outline covers the topic completely, with nothing the reader would expect that is missing.
- The format matches the intent.
- Every question in the outline is a question a real reader asks.
- Every planned link has a real target and descriptive anchor text.

Do not put a plan for publishing, scheduling or distribution into the brief.
